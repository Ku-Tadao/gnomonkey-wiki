**Jal-Zek** (the mage or mager) is one of the most dangerous standard [[Inferno]] monsters. It appears from wave 35 on and hits up to 70 with magic. Gnomonkey calls it the first big scary thing and, in 2022, the queen of the chessboard and the most dangerous piece [c1 c8 c23]. It is by far the most accurate Inferno attacker, roughly twice as dangerous as a ranger, and he says it is never to be tanked [c9 c17 c34 c53]. Its special mechanic is that, instead of a regular attack, it can respawn a monster the player has already killed, which is why the mage is generally killed first [c2 c8 c18]. He advises praying mage at the start of every wave from wave 40 onward [c55]. By 2025 he qualified the kill order: melees come first when there is time pressure, and otherwise the mage [c45 c56].

## Attacks

Inferno mages hit up to 70, compared with 46 for a ranger and 49 for a melee. The mage's hit is roughly twice as accurate as the ranger's, which makes it more dangerous [c17]. In 2025 he says it almost always hits and does not miss, roughly double the average damage of a ranged hit, so it is never worth tanking [c34 c43]. It can put the player to minus 20 health before the next wave starts, so the player should always pray mage after pulling out [c9]. The player starts these waves praying mage [c1].

A mage meleeing the player has a lower accuracy than its magic hit, but is still dangerous. In December 2025 he gave its melee max as around 54; in July 2026 he gave it as around 50 and said it hits very hard because of its accuracy, so the player should not be meleed by one [c48 c57].

Mages attack at the same speed as rangers and are flicked the same way [c28]. The attack is calculated when a flicker appears under the mage, the same visual cue as the ranger hunkering down. The player turns the prayer on just before the flicker starts. In 2026 he adds that the flicker lines up with the audio cue [c1 c7 c36 c54]. He says to learn to lazy flick the mage immediately because it saves prayer and builds the foundation for praying other things while the mage attacks [c46]. Later he notes that a mage's attack takes exactly eight ticks, which matters for flicking [c39], whereas in 2024 he said its normal cycle was four ticks (see Respawn) [c19].

If the player is unsure whether a mage can see them, they should pray mage, since it is better to tank something else than a mage hit [c59]. The mage can see the player from certain tiles, so care is needed when pulling nibblers [c12]. On day one of the Inferno the mage moved around randomly and would run up and bite the player, which lasted about a week [c35].

## Respawn

Instead of a regular attack, a mage can respawn any monster the player has killed (except nibblers) at half health in the centre of the room. A monster can be respawned only once [c2 c24 c37 c52]. In 2025 he gave the example of a bat respawning with 13 health instead of 25 [c44]. The respawn takes eight ticks instead of the standard four, lining up with its four-tick cycle, and does not change that cycle. In 2025 he says the respawn takes exactly two of the mage's attacks [c2 c19 c37].

The chance per attack has been revised: roughly 20% in June 2024, roughly 10% in February and December 2025 [c24 c37 c44].

A respawned blob has half HP, but the blobs it splits into have full health. Gnomonkey notes mage respawns can serve as a tool to regain health with [[Blood Barrage]]: at full health it is usually best to kill the mage first, but when the player needs health it can be good to kill the blob, Blood Barrage it, and let the mage respawn it for more HP [c3 c4]. A respawned monster appears right behind the mage, creating a pillar stack for the player if it is a blob or ranger [c18].

## Kill order

His stated position has been consistent that the mage is usually the priority, with the exceptions changing:

| Date | Position |
|---|---|
| 2021 | Kill the mage first at full health to prevent respawns [c4] |
| 2022 | Always kill the mage first if possible [c8] |
| Jan 2024 | Almost always kill the mage first, because the respawned mob goes right behind it; prefers killing a melee over a blob while the mage is up, since a respawned melee is less annoying [c18 c21] |
| Jun-Oct 2024 | The mage is typically first, but melees can be an exception if they will cause trouble. The only things to kill before a mage are bats and sometimes melees. Never kill a ranger before a mage except in rare waves, and not a blob before the mage [c25 c30 c31 c33] |
| Feb 2025 | Typically the mage first, though sometimes a melee first [c38] |
| Dec 2025 | Melees first because of time pressure, then the mage; with no melee to kill, the mage first [c45] |
| Jul 2026 | The mager first when there is no melee providing time pressure, because killing the other monster first lets it respawn it [c56] |

Killing the ranger first is only fine if the mager is at the side of the pillar and it avoids having to off-tick something [c62]. Off-ticking a mage is only rarely needed to get a cape, and sometimes the ranger is killed first instead [c49]. If a ranger is killed before the mager and gets respawned, the solution is to start from the middle of the pillar, pray the thing in the back, step out when the attack animation is seen, and alternate so the attacks land one tick apart like a blob [c60]. Stepping out the wrong side lets two monsters see the player immediately and hit on the same tick; stepping out the right side works because the mager must first get around the pillar [c61].

## Positioning and tools

* The best place for a mage to spawn is where it can be reached and killed quickly, which Gnomonkey calls a chef's kiss spawn [c47]. On the upper spawn next to the pillar it can be reached with a [[Twisted bow]]; a mage on one particular tile cannot be reached from behind a pillar, the only spot that cannot be reached [c26 c40].
* When the mage is at 34 HP or lower, piping it is better than using the Twisted bow (2023) [c11].
* Standing in the middle of the pillar when monsters drag up makes them get stuck behind it; otherwise the mager comes down the side and removes the player's options [c58].
* The player can stand on a tile that allows a corner trap on a melee when it digs while attacking the mage [c32]. Stepping one tile out lets the mage act as a pillar for corner trapping, giving good insurance few players use [c42]. The space past a mage can serve as a safe area, because the melee cannot come through when the mage is too far down [c41].
* Mage waves are completely free, so Gnomonkey does not even cast at the nibblers, using the pillar collapse to do the damage [c27]. A player who seems to splash on nibblers is probably rolling a zero after passing accuracy; splashing on a nibbler is rare [c20].
* A 40 XP drop after barraging a mage and melee usually means the ranger died and the mage, which has the highest mage defence, is the one left, so he switches to mage [c6].

### Off-ticking

In 2023 he said the big thing at sub-65 is not being afraid to off-tick: send it, and forcing yourself to do it teaches quickly. To off-tick, wait for an attack from the monster closest to the player, step up on that tick, and repeat [c13 c14]. Splashing a mage on purpose stalls it by two ticks, allowing an off-tick, but only if it has not respawned anything [c15]. In 2025 he explains that the game treats a splash on the mage as a melee, so casting and splashing flinches the mage for two ticks as an emergency off-tick; it fails if the mage respawns something after the ranger was killed [c50]. On a bad spawn where the player does not know the splash off-tick, they can simply wait for the melee to dig [c16].

## Double mages

On wave 66 (double mages), just pray mage. The mages can respawn each other, so the player may need to kill four, and a mage whose southwest tile is next to a falling pillar takes massive damage, over a hundred in Gnomonkey's experience [c5]. With two mages, he advises getting both low and finishing them together, and not standing next to the pillar when killing the last mage because the collapse hurts the player for half their current HP [c22 c63]. On the double mage wave, camp mage prayer, get one mage as low as possible, then blowpipe both back to back so one cannot respawn the other [c51]. For a first cape with four and a half restores, he would not flick two mages and would just kill them, as it is too much risk, though he does it all the time himself [c10].
