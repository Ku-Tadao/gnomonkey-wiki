**Deadman Mode** is the PvP-focused game mode that [[Gnomonkey]] played in 2026, first in the Deadman: Annihilation season and then as a member of the Deadman All-Stars roster, a streamed week of play that ended in a live finale in Chicago [c29 c30 c59]. He came to it as a self-described rookie to PvP and a PvM master [c57]. His verdict on Annihilation is mixed: he loves Yama's chaos and thought the brackets were well balanced, but considers the points system a grind, the chests a lame addition and the sigil menu horrendous [c5 c6 c18 c25 c27]. His own All-Stars team, the Odablock Warriors, had a very bad start, a comeback, and a loss in the finale [c38 c56 c59].

## Deadman: Annihilation

### Brackets and immunity

In Annihilation, players are placed in combat-level brackets. Once a player reaches a certain combat level they are booted from the world, cannot gain XP, and can access the bank only a few times before moving to a new world for a different bracket [c7]. Each new bracket entered gives one hour of immunity in which no one can attack the player; Gnomonkey enjoyed min-maxing it, spending his on Barrows, getting nothing, and then starting to kill people [c9]. Brackets enabled pure and other builds, and he had fun in the level 60 to 80 bracket, though he heard the 1 to 60 bracket became horrible after a few days from pker camping [c8]. He felt the brackets were pretty well balanced even though many players complained [c6].

### Points and sigils

Deadman points work like [[Leagues]] points and are spent on sigils, such as an instant crop-harvesting sigil or a sigil giving one tick faster mage attack speed, and on unlocking things like Ancient Magicks and Barrows gloves [c10]. All sigils in Annihilation are bought with points from a sigil shop, whereas Gnomonkey thinks they previously dropped from monsters [c26].

| Source | Points |
|---|---|
| Level-up | 5 [c12] |
| Player kill | 5 per kill, up to 100 kills [c16] |
| One point of damage on a breach boss | 1 (top 16 damage dealers only) [c11 c12] |
| Other sources | Combat achievements, regular diaries, levelling stats, clues [c17] |

A player kill is worth roughly one level-up, which Gnomonkey says is poor when XP rates are ten times normal [c16]. A sigil makes clues a single step, and the best method for points is skilling, raids (ignoring purples for combat achievements) and clues [c17]. He calls this grinding boring and not dangerous, like Leagues but in a PvP game mode [c18]. He still considers points essential, because without them a player cannot keep up when moving up brackets: others attack faster, have much higher accuracy and unlimited prayer points [c19].

Breach points were meant to be one point per point of damage dealt, but only the top 16 damage dealers receive any, which Jagex did not mention when marketing it. With over a thousand players at breaches, reaching the top 16 was very hard, so breach points were barely obtainable [c11 c13]. Jagex said on the Wednesday that it planned to raise the cutoff to the top 100 players, which Gnomonkey says is still not many given the crowds [c14].

The sigil menu is the most used menu in the mode, and Gnomonkey calls it horrendous: it is not alphabetical, sigils appear in random order, the filters are haphazard and there is no search. He says it needs a search function, more filters and proper sorting, as finding a sigil like Litheness takes around 30 seconds to two minutes of scrolling [c27 c28].

### Breaches

Breaches spawn many random bosses roughly every seven hours (as Gnomonkey recalls). Killing them gives PvP-helping drops such as emblems and supply drops, plus points [c3]. The big addition was [[Yama]], who spams meatballs that one-shot players; Gnomonkey calls them maybe the best addition to any Deadman mode ever and loves the chaos of players wiped out in droves [c4 c5]. On day one or two his client lagged so badly he could barely move, and the meatballs turned invisible because of the number of players, killing people with invisible meatballs [c15].

### Chests

Deadman chests are announced in chat. A player opens one by sitting at it doing a thieving-style opening animation for 60 seconds, after which they get strong loot, which can include the Dragon sword and the Khopesh [c20 c21]. Gnomonkey says basically only players with ultimate-level setups or Giga Max risks bother, since sitting defenceless for 60 seconds is dangerous. He considers the chests a lame addition and says most players ignored them because the loot was not worth the trouble [c22 c25]. Two exploits are described: a bug let players log out underneath the chest and log back in inside it, where they could still open it and no one could hit them, and players arrived with around ten alts and shot anyone opening the chest with bone crossbows, so whoever had the most alts won the chest [c23 c24].

## Deadman Mode Allstars (June 2026)

Gnomonkey announced on 27 May 2026 that Deadman Mode was coming at the beginning of June and that he was participating. On 29 May he said he was on the Deadman All-Stars roster and would be on stage in Chicago in the middle of June, with streams for the whole week [c29 c30]. The event involved 120 hours of streaming over a week, and he wanted to give full context because many viewers jumped between streams and clips [c58]. His team was the Odablock Warriors [c39 c59].

### Breach strategy

For the fifth breach the Odablock Warriors used a calculated redemption plan from Evscape, with Odablock as main caller, Raikesy as secondary caller, and Dubie Dobies and Gnomonkey as snipers [c39]. The team aims to be the MVP on each monster so that tier five loot goes to them [c40]. Tactical notes from the breaches:

* The music lulls when there are no monsters, signalling that the team must wait for the next spawn [c41].
* Monsters can aggro unexpectedly, so the team stands on tiles with line of sight so they can shoot from safe positions [c42].
* When luring a monster, do not mage it too early because the team wants to keep it in their position; if a teammate does mage it, use blood spells [c43].
* Dragging monsters north toward single-combat tiles improves a player's odds of living, since rival teams cannot gang up there [c44]. A single-combat tile also means the player standing on it cannot do damage, so Gnomonkey offered to take aggro and tank while teammates dealt damage [c46].
* An area far east of the fairy ring is out of range of the Hunter's Guild, where the Dino Nuggets bank, so monsters pulled there can be farmed with less risk [c45].

### Escapes

In the first breach of Deadman Mode Season 3, Gnomonkey escaped a chase but was frozen by a Westham Weasels player who teleported in on him [c31]. Advice from that episode: with three minutes left on a skull he could return with his tier five sacks and become stronger again [c32]; a teammate suggested the team keep committing to random targets so the chasing clans cannot concentrate on one player [c33]; he was told to wait out his skull before teleporting [c34]; Validor teleports are free for teleporting back in [c35]; and after an escape the team can use a home teleport once the pursuers give up, logging back in and teleporting after the one-minute window [c37]. When Gnomonkey ran out of teleport charges and could not teleport or log off while chased, he stalled the enemy while the rest of the team moved [c36].

### Breach 5 and the Inferno mission

The Odablock Warriors won the fifth breach after a starting plan that went badly and strategies that did not work left them with 40 deaths over a few days, which the description calls the worst start in Deadman history [c38]. When the Westham Weasels rushed, Gnomonkey made the call to do a full tank so teammates could escape and farm more tier fives. He lost the trinket but said he earned points for prayers [c47].

The team had no gear for the whole week, which Gnomonkey says made the [[Inferno]] mission extremely dangerous [c48]. The team had agreed beforehand to take a Corrupted Void Waker as the reward [c52]. He did not know whether the mission needed only the kill count or the [[Fire cape]] too, hoped it was not both because the team does not share the cape, and was glad the kill count was enough; the team confirmed it registered after Zuk died [c51 c53]. After the kill, a teammate told him not to celebrate on the spot but to go to Draynor, then grab a ring of returning and go to the Grand Exchange, because enemy teams were coming [c50]. In hindsight he says the Corrupted weapon was not really worth the risk, but the moment itself made it worth it. He hoped the run would redeem the team's last breach [c49 c54].

### Finale and review

Before the finale, Gnomonkey said his team was not in the best standing but had a big comeback, with lower odds than the Westham Weasels but a genuine shot at winning. He planned to practise hard and then make a video on his thoughts as a PvP rookie and PvM master, plus an overall review [c56 c57]. His team did not win the finale in Chicago [c59].

Details from the event as he reports them on 25 June:

* The 120-hour play cap over eight days meant 18-hour play days for his team versus about 14 hours for other teams, plus 20 to 30 hours logged out discussing plans, for an estimated 150 hours total [c60]. He streamed 18 hours a day on usually two to four hours of sleep, lost 13 pounds from stress, and fell asleep face down on the carpet for three hours after the last day [c61].
* Players voted on a stream delay and voted no. Gnomonkey voted no but says he would vote yes next time, even for a three-minute chat delay [c62].
* During the victim mission a ref made a bad call and everybody killed someone who did not have the totem the ref claimed they had, and Mammal was punished for dropping the totem. Gnomonkey says refs let captains decide punishments, that captains should not, and that all teams should have been punished equally [c63 c64].
* At the Varlamore breach the Odablock Warriors held a very solid position, lost a couple of people to the Westham Weasels at the end, and got many tier five totems, finally getting Odablock and Dubies their prayers [c65]. In the Victim Has It mission they sat on the outskirts casting barrages at clumps of players and still had all five alive at the end, and Perp stole the VLS [c66].
* Odablock killed Lake in the last hour of the week, which got the team six blood money for brews and venge trinkets. Gnomonkey says he saved the team about 10 of their 40 blood money by pulling prayer scrolls from Chambers of Xeric [c67].
