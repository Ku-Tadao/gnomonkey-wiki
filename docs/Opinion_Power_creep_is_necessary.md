**Opinion: Power creep is necessary** is an opinion topic that Gnomonkey covers in 1 video on 2022-05-25. This article is a stub: his statements are grouped by type, repeated ones merged.

## Gnomonkey's opinion

* Gnomonkey says, as a former RuneScape 3 player who has seen extreme power creep, that power creep is less of a problem than the Old School community makes it out to be.[^SO4k9z1A8-0-0]
* Gnomonkey says any update that adds something devalues something else: the blowpipe devalued crossbows, Twisted bow further devalued the blowpipe, and even Guardians of the Rift removing the delay after crafting essence or shift-click dropping counts.[^SO4k9z1A8-0-26]
* Gnomonkey says power creep is a problem because it devalues content: old bosses become easier and items become irrelevant to grind for economically.[^SO4k9z1A8-0-55]
* Gnomonkey argues that every update ever added was power creep in some way, so there is no solution but to either stop adding updates and let everything decay, or accept a small amount of power creep while curbing it in various ways as other MMOs do.[^SO4k9z1A8-0-113]
* Gnomonkey says a small amount of power creep is not only inevitable but necessary to keep a game fun and interesting, as long as it is maintained with checks and balances; without a Twisted bow in Chambers or a Scythe in Theatre of Blood those raids would be much less fun.[^SO4k9z1A8-0-430]
* Gnomonkey says the OSRS team recently decided it is OK to start adding actual best-in-slot items like Torva and Masori armour; he says when the team struggles to find enticing rewards comparable to existing ones, that weapon or armour tier is fully fleshed out and it is time to move on.[^SO4k9z1A8-0-462]
* Gnomonkey says raids need solid rewards to be interesting and engaging, and that gear choices (how many switches you can afford, and the impact of each) keep raids engaging; e.g. one challenge mode player might bring Torva for BGS specs while others use Inquisitor's to land hammer specs.[^SO4k9z1A8-0-462]

## Trivia

* Gnomonkey defines power creep (reading from Google) as the gradual unbalancing of a game due to successive releases of new content leaving older content underpowered; the community commonly calls it devaluing.[^SO4k9z1A8-0-26]
